CAP 600 — Capstone Studio: Thesis & Defense

The degree's final instrument: a real AI product, built, measured, packaged, and defended — the artifact that converts the entire program into a hiring signal. GTH 580 (Career Launch) runs in parallel during the final weeks.

Course objectives
Scope a 15–20-hour build with a pre-registered evaluation design.
Ship a working AI product and run the eval cycle with before/after numbers.
Write a 10–12 page thesis traceable to every portfolio artifact.
Defend it in a 15-minute recorded presentation, then produce the transition kit.
Modules
M1Proposal

A capstone demonstrates judgment, not engineering. The scoping rule: narrow enough to finish, deep enough to show. A document-Q&A assistant, a meeting-notes summarizer with action extraction, a support-triage bot — all sufficient. The one-page brief must cite earlier courses: problem (PM 501 evidence), users, scope in/out, success metrics (DATA 530), eval plan (AIPM 520), cost model (STRAT 540), risks (RSK 550). If a section cannot cite a prior course, that is a signal about the course — or about the idea.

The load-bearing decision is evaluation design before code: pre-register the golden set (25+ cases) and the success bar before writing a line, so "done" means "measured," not "demoed." Pre-registration is what makes the final eval report honest — you cannot move the goalposts after seeing results if the goalposts were published first. The FDE twist stands: the strongest capstone is a real Forward-Deployed-shaped problem — a working prototype your team could show a client. That is not extra credit; it is the point.

M2Build & measure

The build discipline: no-code + API scripts, or Python with heavy AI assistance — using an LLM to write code is what modern PMs do; reading every line it writes is what makes them PMs. You are the reviewer in the loop (RSK 550), not the passenger: every line the model produces is untrusted code until you understand it. Then the loop that is the entire point: run the golden set → score → fix the worst failure mode → re-run. Record everything: eval before/after, cost per interaction, latency, three user-visible examples of the improvement.

The eval report is the capstone's center of gravity — a working product without measured results is a hobby. If your numbers embarrass you, good: the fix-and-re-run cycle is the evidence interviewers most want to hear about, because it demonstrates the operating discipline from AIPM 520 — quality as a loop, not a launch checkbox. Two extra disciplines: log cost and latency as first-class metrics (STRAT 540), and keep a failure log of the three worst failures and how each was diagnosed.

M3Packaging & external review

Hiring managers don't read résumés; they read evidence. The packaging stack: the case study (problem → process → solution → results → what you'd do differently, in STAR discipline, with real numbers), the 3-minute demo video with no dead air (talking through your own product is a job interview in miniature), the portfolio index (one page: who you are, the asset map, five best artifacts with one-line pitches), and the LinkedIn refresh — a headline with PM intent and the case study posted.

Then the program's external examiner: send the index to a working PM or hiring manager and get their read. Honors requires their pass — and regardless of honors, the review is the calibration this program cannot give you alone. The test of packaging: a stranger understands the case study in three minutes. If they can't, the problem is the packaging, not the work — revise until the stranger test passes.

M4Defense & transition

The written thesis (10–12 pages) follows the arc of the program: problem & evidence → design → feasibility & architecture → evaluation with real numbers → economics → risk → retrospective. Every claim must be traceable to a portfolio artifact — the thesis is the synthesis, the artifacts are the proof. The 15-minute recorded defense answers five questions, including the two that separate the prepared from the lucky: "what is the most likely way this fails in production, and how would you detect it?" and "if compute costs doubled, what would you do?"

Then the transition kit: three STAR stories mined from your strategist/operator career and translated into PM vocabulary (the M1 asset map, turned into narrative), a résumé written in outcomes ("led X, defined success as Y, shipped Z"), and the one-page 30/60/90 — 30 days learning the business and users, 60 days owning a feature end-to-end, 90 days driving a measured outcome. The 30/60/90 gets you hired because it shows you arrive already doing the job — which, after this course, is true.

Final · checklist · key terms

Course final — the thesis & defense

The capstone final is already fully specified in the exam book: written thesis (10–12 pages), 15-minute recorded defense, portfolio review. This section is your pre-defense checklist.

Pre-defense checklist
  1. Every thesis claim is traceable to a portfolio artifact — walk the links yourself before submitting.
  2. Your eval report has real before/after numbers, including cost per interaction and latency.
  3. You can answer "what is the most likely way this fails in production, and how would you detect it?" with a mechanism, not a hope.
  4. You can answer "if compute costs doubled, what would you do?" with the sensitivity from your cost model.
  5. The stranger test passed: someone unfamiliar with the project restated your case study correctly in three minutes.
  6. The external reviewer (Module 3) has seen the portfolio index; their feedback is incorporated.

Rubric summary

Thesis 50% · defense 30% · portfolio review 20%. Full rubric in exams.md. An "A" requires the external examiner's pass — treat their read as the real grade.
