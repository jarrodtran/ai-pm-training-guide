GTH 580 — Career Launch: Portfolio, Interviews & Transition

The conversion layer: packaging the portfolio into evidence, mastering the interview loops, running a job search that works, and arriving with a 30/60/90 that shows you already do the job.

Course objectives
Package the portfolio: case study, demo, index, LinkedIn.
Master the three interview loops — product sense, execution, behavioral.
Run a job search: sourcing, networking, referrals, offer evaluation.
Arrive: a one-page 30/60/90 for the first AI PM role.
Modules
M1Portfolio packaging

Hiring managers don't read résumés; they read evidence. The packaging stack, in order of leverage: the case study — problem → process → solution → results → what you'd do differently, in STAR discipline, with real numbers from your eval report; the 3-minute demo video — talking through your own product, no dead air, because a job interview is this, live; the portfolio index — one page: who you are, the asset map, five best artifacts with one-line pitches; and the LinkedIn refresh — a headline with PM intent, the case study posted, the network engaged.

The test is the stranger test: a stranger understands the case study in three minutes. If they can't, the packaging is the problem, not the work — and the fix is ruthless editing, not more content. One judgment call to make early: which five artifacts carry the weight (the starred list in the master syllabus — eval suite, feasibility framework, production eval plan, cost model, capstone eval report)? The index should lead with those, not with the easiest ones. This module runs against CAP 600's packaging week — one stack, two grades.

M2The interview loops

The AI PM interview tests three loops. Product sense: "design an AI feature for X" — the graders want structure, stated assumptions, feasibility judgment, metrics, and risks; you now have a framework for each (AIPM 520 M1, PM 501 M5, RSK 550 M1). Execution: "how would you prioritize / measure / de-risk?" — RICE, guardrails, risk registers: your portfolio is the evidence. Behavioral: "tell me about a time…" — STAR stories ending in numbers, mined from your strategist/operator career and translated into PM vocabulary — the asset map from PM 501 M1, turned into narrative.

The killer question — "walk me through how you'd decide whether a feature should use AI" — now has a two-minute answer with a real framework: comparative feasibility → data check → evaluation design → cost/latency → risk. The practice protocol is the training: record yourself answering ten questions, listen back, repeat. Verbalized judgment under pressure is the skill being tested; it is trainable, and this is the training. Two common failure modes to preempt: answers without numbers (every behavioral story needs a metric), and frameworks recited without application (always land the framework on the specific question).

M3Job search mechanics

A job search is a pipeline, and you are the PM of it. Sourcing: target 20–30 companies across your tracks — Enterprise/FDE, big tech, venture — scored on a scorecard (role scope, AI maturity, learning surface, comp, mission fit). Networking: the referral is the channel that works; reach out with specificity — their work plus your evidence — not spray. The funnel: track applications → screens → loops → offers with conversion data and fix the leaky stage (a strong loop-to-offer rate with a weak screen rate is a résumé problem, not an interview problem).

Offer evaluation is where PMs most often leave money and leverage on the table: compare total comp honestly — base, bonus, and equity mechanics (strike price, vesting, dilution basics — an offer with a high strike price on a late-stage company is worth less than its face value), plus the AI-role premium the market is paying. But weigh learning surface and scope heavily: the first AI PM role is an apprenticeship as much as a job, and the best apprenticeship may pay less. The negotiable: everything. Ask — the ask is the feature, not the bug.

M4The first 90 days

The 30/60/90 is the arrival document that gets you hired and sets you up. 30 days — learn: the business, the users, the metrics, the model stack; interview everyone; make no changes yet; the goal is to understand the system before touching it. 60 days — own: take a small feature end-to-end — discovery, spec, ship, measure — because a completed loop builds more trust than a hundred good opinions. 90 days — drive: move a metric with evidence, propose the roadmap, and establish the eval-and-risk posture for your product area (your AIPM 520 production eval plan, adapted to their stack, is your first contribution).

The plan fits on one page and names: the people you'll learn from (by role), the feature you'll own, the metric you'll move, and the risks you'll watch. It shows you arrive already doing the job — which, after this program, is true. The final move is the one this whole degree was built for: when you walk into that first AI PM role, the person interviewing you will be interviewing someone who has already done the work once — on their own time, under their own honor code, with the artifacts to prove it.
