Program description

A degree-caliber program in the tradition of the Harvard Business School case method and the Stanford graduate seminar: seven core courses, three electives, four real cases, six primary papers, and a defended capstone thesis. Built for a strategist/operator moving into AI product management — but general enough for any ambitious professional. Students graduate able to run discovery, write specs engineers can build from, judge when AI is the wrong tool, design production eval systems, model cost, own risk, and defend a shipped, measured product.

Method & assessment

The pedagogy. Reading-intensive, artifact-producing, case-driven. Every week produces a portfolio artifact — a prompt library, an eval suite, a cost model, a risk register — that doubles as the graduate's interview evidence. AI is allowed everywhere; every deliverable carries an AI-use declaration and must survive questioning. That supervision is the skill.

The rhythm. 5–10 hours/week: ~2.5h pre-class preparation, ~2h problem set, ~2h deliverable, ~0.5–1h self-assessment. ~44 weeks total; compressible to ~6 months at 15+ hrs/week.

Grading. Per course: problem sets 40% · case memos 20% · course project 30% · self-assessment 10%. Capstone: thesis 50% · defense 30% · portfolio 20%.

Honors. Certificate at 3.3+ GPA; honors requires the external examiner — a working PM or hiring manager reviewing the portfolio — to pass. The grade that matters is given by the real world.

The capstone. A real AI product, built in 15–20 hours, measured with a pre-registered eval suite, defended in a 15-minute recorded presentation.

Materials & tuition

Three used paperbacks — The Mom Test (~$10), Cracking the PM Interview (~$15), Inspired (~$15) — are the entire budget. Lectures are the field's canonical free talks (Karpathy, 3Blue1Brown, DeepLearning.AI); papers are on arXiv; tools run on free tiers and local models (Ollama, Colab, Kaggle). Tuition is time: ~280–310 hours of deliberate, artifact-producing work.
