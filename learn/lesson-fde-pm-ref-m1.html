1.1 The core PM mandate (outcomes → scope, not “how”)

Your job is to turn a customer business outcome into an engineering scope with clear constraints, acceptance, and owners — without dictating implementation.

You own FDE / Tech Lead owns Client owns
Problem statement with a number and a named owner Technical approach inside constraints Business outcome definition, data access, process change
Instrument choice (AI vs deterministic pipeline vs change-mgmt) Architecture, estimates, build Baseline metrics, golden-set authorship for acceptance
Acceptance criteria & “done” before build Eval harness / implementation Sign-off, security review, production entitlements
Shield / triage / price of scope creep Execution of priced work Prioritization among their stakeholders
Harvest / productization decision Pattern extraction into core Consent if their bespoke becomes a product

Mental models (from M0 / SVPG spine):

  • Product vs feature teams: Lead for outcomes. Feature-factory mode is the default failure when you lead deployers.
  • PM ≠ project management: Sequencing delivery is not the same as choosing what creates product value.
  • Product-management theater: Title says PM; job is backlog and coordination. Name it and exit it.
  • Dev vs Delta: Deployment context defines the FDE. You own the *missing product decisions*, not their craft.

Translate outcomes without dictating how:

  1. Write the outcome as a sentence with a metric, baseline, target, and measurement source.
  2. Name constraints (data residency, air-gap, latency budget, compliance) as one-way doors where needed.
  3. Require an ADR for those doors; let FDE draft body; you own decision + consequences.
  4. Put acceptance in the SOW before build (M4 spine). Contested acceptance later is a PM miss.

Example: “Cut median policy-ticket handle time from 11 to ≤7 minutes by date X, measured from ticket timestamps, n≥300.” You do not specify chunk size or vector DB. You specify entitlements on retrieval, citation requirement, and human review on high-stakes slice.

1.2 PRD vs technical spec vs SOW (ownership map)

These are different instruments. Collapsing them creates disputes at the fragile moment.

Artifact Primary author Answers Delivery guarantee?
PRD (or product brief) PM Why / who / success metrics / non-goals / UX intent No — intent, not a contract
Technical spec / design FDE / Tech Lead How / components / interfaces / failure modes Internal agreement among builders
SOW PM (with legal/sales as needed) What is in/out, access, acceptance, change control, commercial terms Yes — external commitment
Discovery RFC PM + FDE lead What we learned, instrument choice, risks, proposed SOW shape Pre-commit alignment
ADR FDE drafts; PM owns decision One durable choice + consequences Decision record, not a schedule

Rules:

  • Ambiguity in a SOW is not “flexibility”; it is dispute inventory.
  • Demo illusion: a demo can look excellent. Pre-agreed thresholds on a client-authored golden set are the defense.
  • Fifth-stakeholder discipline: procurement, legal, security, data governance, IT ops — bring them into discovery as informed participants, not late vetoes.

1.3 Enterprise discovery & scoping (constraints before commit)

Ask ≠ problem. “We want an AI assistant” is a request. Behind it is a process, a queue, a cost, a person’s week.

Three diagnoses (first meeting):

Diagnosis Signals Correct PM response
AI problem Unstructured input; fuzzy judgment; high volume + ambiguity Retrieval + model + eval suite; scope eval before build
Deterministic pipeline Structured rules; exactness required (money, eligibility) Connectors, CDC, transforms — do not put a model in the exact decision path
Change-management failure Tool exists; adoption is the issue No new system — sponsorship, process, incentives

Most “AI projects” are two of three stacked. Name and price each layer separately.

JTBD / shadowing: Official process is fiction. Watch the spreadsheet, chat thread, printer. The gap is where integration complexity and adoption risk live (HBR JTBD; Kotter for change anatomy).

Constraint hunt (before commit):

  • Legacy: what systems are source of truth; batch vs real-time; undocumented APIs
  • Compliance / residency / air-gap: what may leave the estate
  • Throughput / latency budgets: what “fast enough” means in their SLA language
  • Identity: SSO/SAML/OIDC; entitlements on retrieval (permission-aware)
  • Data quality: dirty data is a scope item, not a surprise in week six

Artifacts: Discovery RFC → feasibility → SOW. Instrument triage on the whiteboard. Climb buy → adapt → build.

1.4 Backlog health: customization vs platform

Pressure If unmanaged PM counter
Client one-offs Permanent forks; margin death Price: config / bespoke displacing X / product candidate
Utilization north star Harvest starved Publish dual clocks: client dates vs product quarters
“Just this once” Scope-creep tax 3-day triage ritual; change control in SOW
Third same ask Still bespoke Three-client rule → pattern ledger + productization memo

Reuse rate (with quality/satisfaction guardrails) beats utilization as the team KPI. Bespoke is inventory with carrying cost, not pure waste — and not free.
